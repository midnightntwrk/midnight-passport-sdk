# E07.04 Rebuild the coin store from the chain

**Epic:** E07 · Payments · **Milestone:** 2 Account management and core actions · **Step:** 11  
**Epic issue:** [#50](https://github.com/midnightntwrk/midnight-passport-sdk/issues/50)  
**Packages:** core  
**Readiness:** ready · **Sheet title:** none (new story)

## Description

The coin store is a local cache. It is not in any backup: the SDK requirements say Passport makes it again with the viewing key. So this scan is how a new device, a wiped browser or a recovered account gets its coins. It is the primary restore of the store.

E07.01 (inbox, coin store and deposits) makes one received coin spendable as it arrives. This story does the same for a whole account from nothing: every entry in the inbox, checked against one block of chain data.

Proposed by the provider. This story is not on the board.

In scope: The scan: walk the inbox with E07.01; for each candidate locate its commitment and tree position; check that the coin exists and is not spent. It ports the logic of `contract/src/wallet/chain-recovery.ts` in the passport repo, with `contract/CHAIN-RECOVERY.md` in the passport repo and `recoverHoldings` in `contract/src/wallet/account.ts` in the passport repo as references. The chain adapter is a rewrite over the indexer seam, with `contract/src/wallet/sdk-chain-recovery.ts` in the passport repo as reference: that file opens its own sockets. A report per coin: live, spent, or unknown with the reason. The whole report refers to one block, and says how much of the inbox it covered. The indexer reads this story needs: a snapshot pinned to one block (the block, the contract state and the contract's part of the commitment tree at that block); the contract's shielded receive events with their tree positions; lookups of spent markers up to that block; and the account's action history. The read belongs to the indexer seam, whose owner is E04.06 (indexer seam); this story names what it needs and does not add a second client. A cursor, so a long scan can stop and continue on the same block. A merge of the report into an existing store that does not bring spent coins back.

Out of scope: Getting the viewing key onto a new device, which is E19.05 (new-device bootstrap) and the recovery epics. Grant openings, which are other private data and are not on chain. Unshielded funds, which need no rebuild because the account's public state has them.

## Dependencies

**Blocked by:** E07.01 Inbox entries, the coin store, and deposits into an account; E07.03 Send from the account to a wallet address; E04.06 Indexer seam: transaction status and account state. E07.03 (send to a wallet address) makes the sends whose change coins and entries the scan has to locate. E04.06 (indexer seam) has to carry the four reads listed above.  
**Unlocks:** E19.05 New-device bootstrap: keys and state. A new device, a wiped browser and a recovered account all call it once they have the viewing key: the wiped-device path of E06.03 (returning-user resume), and the state restore after a guardian recovery. Those board stories do not list it today.

## Acceptance criteria

- On a local node, an account receives three shielded deposits through the builder of E07.01 (inbox, coin store and deposits) and sends part of one with E07.03 (send to a wallet address). A fresh client with only the open function rebuilds a store with the same holdings as the original, and then spends a rebuilt coin on the node.
- An inbox entry that describes a coin that does not exist is reported as unknown and is not counted.
- A scan that is stopped and continued gives the same report as one uninterrupted scan.
- With the indexer unreachable the scan gives a typed error. It never reports an empty account.
- The test records the time and the number of indexer requests for inboxes of 10 and 100 entries.

- What does the indexer operator learn from the scan's queries? Not analysed.
