# E07.03 Send from the account to a wallet address

**Epic:** E07 · Payments · **Milestone:** 2 Account management and core actions · **Step:** 10  
**Epic issue:** [#50](https://github.com/midnightntwrk/midnight-passport-sdk/issues/50)  
**Packages:** core, owner  
**Readiness:** ready · **Sheet title:** none (new story)

## Description

The owner moves value out of the account to an ordinary Midnight wallet: unshielded funds to a user address, shielded funds to a shielded address. This is `pay` to an address, in `core`, as the architecture's examples have it.

A shielded send spends a whole coin and gives back change that only this client can describe. Two things follow. The client has to survive stopping between sending the transaction and recording the change, so the send keeps a journal. And the custody spec says the client must write the change coin's description to the inbox (MIP-0012, section 6.5). The owner's withdraw circuit does not do that itself, so a send with change is two owner calls.

Proposed by the provider. This story is not on the board. It is the withdrawal half of board story "Transfers via deposit calls".

In scope: Address parsing in `core`: one parsed shielded address gives both halves, the coin public key and the encryption public key (rule 6, a full shielded address). It checks the network and returns typed errors. An account contract address is refused as a payment target, with a pointer to E07.02 (pay another Passport, shielded) (rule 3, deposit, not address). `core`: the call builder for `withdraw_unshielded` and `withdraw_shielded`, and `pay` to an address on top of it. The builder takes a signer. `owner` adds only what needs the owner key: the passkey signer of E04.11 (passkey on the p256 arm) and the prompt. Coin selection from the store of E07.01 (inbox, coin store and deposits). The contract spends one coin per call, so an amount larger than every single coin of that color gets a typed error. The change journal, in `core`: the intent is written before the transaction is sent, and the change coin is recorded with its tree position once the transaction is final. On start, unfinished entries are reconciled against the chain. It ports from `contract/src/wallet/change-journal.ts` in the passport repo, which has an owner mode and a grant mode, so E15.03 (grant-key call pipeline) uses the same journal. `contract/src/wallet/sdk-change-adapter.ts` in the passport repo and `contract/DURABLE-CHANGE.md` in the passport repo are references; the adapter is rewritten over the SDK's seams. This journal is the one mechanism for surviving a stop. Later payment stories record their steps in it and do not keep a second state. The change entry. After a send with change, the client seals the change coin's description to the account's own key and appends it to the inbox with a second owner call, before any later inbox write (MIP-0012, section 6.5). With a passkey that is a second prompt and a second proof. The journal keeps the append pending until it lands, and the next send does it first when it is still open. A "pending payments" read: what the journal holds that is not finished, with the step each one is at. The screens of E08 (Passport app: the account) use this read and nothing else to show work in progress. The store changes only after the transaction ends with `SucceedEntirely` (rule 8, write-back after `SucceedEntirely`). A partial success leaves the store as it was and returns a typed "not spent, fee charged" result. A local write that fails is kept and tried again. Retries follow the action table of E04.01 (sponsor policy) through E04.04 (broadcast adapter), which also keeps one owner transaction in flight per account. A refusal for a coin already spent is final. A stale call is rebuilt and proved again; when its signature no longer holds, the flow raises a typed "needs approval again" event. The indexer read this story needs: the tree positions of a transaction's outputs, for the change coin. The read belongs to the indexer seam, whose owner is E04.06 (indexer seam); this story names what it needs and does not add a second client.

Out of scope: Paying an account, which is E07.02 and E07.06 (pay another Passport, unshielded). Spending under a grant, which is E15.03. Merging coins.

## Acceptance criteria

- On a local node, with a p256 account made by E04.12 (wave deploy) and a test token, funded through the deposit builder of E07.01 (inbox, coin store and deposits): an unshielded send arrives at a wallet address and the account's public balance for that color drops by the amount.
- A shielded send of part of a coin arrives at a wallet. The change coin is in the store with its tree position, and the inbox has one more entry, which opens to that change coin. A second send spends the change coin.
- The process is killed at each journal stage, including between the spend and the append. After a restart the store matches the chain, the pending append is completed, and no coin is lost or counted twice.
- The pending payments read lists a send from the moment its intent is written until it is finished, and nothing after.
- A send that ends in a partial success leaves the store unchanged and returns the typed result. The test forces it with a competing state change.
- The address parser passes vectors for: a valid shielded address (both halves), the wrong network, an unshielded address where a shielded one is needed, and an account contract address.
- A dependency test shows `pay`, the call builder and the journal are in `core`, and that `owner` adds only the signer.
- The tests assert the passkey prompts when no re-prompt occurs: one for an unshielded send or a whole-coin send, two for a shielded send with change. They record prompts, transactions and seconds.

## Open questions

- For the contract owner: the grant form of the shielded withdraw takes the change entry as an argument and appends it in the same call. The owner form does not, so an owner send with change costs a second prompt and proof. Should the owner circuit take the change entry too? That is a contract change: it changes verifier keys.
- Not measured: any withdraw signed by a passkey, or the append that follows it. In tests, withdraws ran with JubJub and k256 owner keys only.
- Not measured: the time of a send with a passkey. In tests, other passkey owner calls took 25 to 60 s of proving, and a send with change is two of them.
- Not measured with a passkey: a deposit from someone else landing while a send is being proved. In tests with other key types, the held call was refused and had to be rebuilt. With a passkey that may mean one more prompt.
- An amount larger than any one coin: merge coins first, or several sends? Not designed.
- No Foundation story owns "make an owner call for a named circuit" as a helper. E04.12 (wave deploy) does it once in its test. This story writes the call builder for its own circuits in `core`; should it be the general one?
- Things like stricter hex and length checks in the reference wallet code are still in an open PR (passport#199). The port should start from the merged version.
- A draft experiment adds per-asset spend policies to the account contract (passport#194). If it is adopted, a send has to resolve and prove the policy too.
