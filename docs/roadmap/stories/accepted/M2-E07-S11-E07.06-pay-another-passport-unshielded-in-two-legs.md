# E07.06 Pay another Passport: unshielded, in two legs

**Epic:** E07 · Payments · **Milestone:** 2 Account management and core actions · **Step:** 11  
**Epic issue:** [#50](https://github.com/midnightntwrk/midnight-passport-sdk/issues/50)  
**Packages:** core, owner  
**Readiness:** to discuss implementation · **Sheet title:** none (new story)

## Description

Pay another Passport in unshielded funds. The account's unshielded withdraw pays a user address and cannot pay a contract, and the recipient's balance changes only through `deposit_unshielded`. So this payment is two transactions: the payer withdraws to an address of its own, and then deposits from that address into the recipient's account. Only the first needs the passkey.

Proposed by the provider. This story is not on the board. It is the unshielded half of board story "Transfers via deposit calls".

To discuss before it starts. The flow needs a wallet key that the client holds between two transactions, and no story says where that key comes from. Whether V1 needs unshielded payments between Passports at all is also open.

In scope: `core`: `pay` to an account in unshielded funds. Leg one is the unshielded send of E07.03 (send to a wallet address) to the payer's own address. Leg two is a `deposit_unshielded` built with the builder of E07.01 (inbox, coin store and deposits). Both legs are steps of one entry in the journal of E07.03. There is no second state. The pending payments read of that story shows the payment and the leg it is at. When leg two keeps failing, the value is deposited back into the payer's own account. Every stopping point has a typed outcome. The wallet key between the legs. It is created once and kept in the local persistence of E04.05 (platform adapters), so a restart reads it back. That is a placeholder for throwaway test accounts: where the key really comes from has to be decided in E04.20 (account secrets spec), which has no row for it. The recipient is resolved as in E07.02 (pay another Passport, shielded): by name, or by an identifier until names exist.

Out of scope: Shielded payments, which are E07.02. The send to a wallet address, which is E07.03. NIGHT: the SDK requirements say no circuit moves it between two accounts.

## Dependencies

**Blocked by:** E07.03 Send from the account to a wallet address; E07.01 Inbox entries, the coin store, and deposits into an account; E04.20 Account secrets and deploy inputs without a passkey-derived secret (spec and ADR); E04.18 Name-to-ACC resolution. E04.20 (account secrets spec) has to give the wallet key a source before this is used outside tests. E04.18 (name resolution) is needed for paying by name only.  
**Unlocks:** nothing on the board. E08.03 (send screens) shows this payment when it exists, but does not wait for it.

## Acceptance criteria

- On a local node, with two p256 accounts and a test token: an unshielded payment from A raises B's public balance by the amount, in 2 transactions and 1 passkey prompt.
- The process is killed between the legs. After a restart the wallet key is read back from local persistence and leg two completes.
- With leg two forced to fail, the value returns to A's account and the journal entry ends as "returned".
- The pending payments read shows the payment from leg one until it is paid or returned.
- The by-name path is tested with a stub resolver until E04.18 (name resolution) lands.

## Open questions

- Does V1 need unshielded payments between Passports? If not, this story waits.
- The wallet key between the legs: where does it come from and where is it kept? If it is lost between the legs, the value is stranded. It also means wallet state in the page, which E04.04 (broadcast adapter) set out to avoid.
- Who pays the fee of leg two? E04.01 (sponsor policy) lists calls to account circuits as paid. This deposit is made by a plain wallet key, not by an owner. To confirm, with its limit per caller.
- Not measured: this payment with passkey accounts. In tests with other key types, an unshielded deposit took 21 s on a desktop prover, on top of the withdrawal.
