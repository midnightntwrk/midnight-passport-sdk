# E04.04 adapter-broadcast: sponsor payment, submit, follow, resubmit

**Epic:** E04 · Account deployment · **Milestone:** 1 Foundation · **Step:** 6  
**Epic issue:** [#47](https://github.com/midnightntwrk/midnight-passport-sdk/issues/47)  
**Packages:** adapter-broadcast, core  
**Readiness:** ready · **Sheet title:** Broadcasting

## Description

Take a proven transaction, have the sponsor pay its fee, submit it, and follow it until it is in a block or final. When the node refuses it, rebuild and pay again instead of resending old bytes. A user wallet holds no DUST on any path (rule 14, no user-held DUST).

Board title: adapter-broadcast: submit, finality, resubmit. This story now also holds the "sponsor pays the fees" half of board story E04.09 (sponsored deploy and starting balance).

In scope: The Broadcast and Fees seams implemented in one adapter: send the proven, unbound transaction to the sponsor, submit what comes back. Pay just before sending. Never collect several paid transactions and then send them. Two wait levels the caller picks: "in a block" and "final". Bounded waits for both. The action table from E04.01 (sponsor policy): retry, rebuild and pay again, or stop. Reading the node's refusal from the error's `cause` chain, where midnight-js 5.0.0 puts it. One transaction in flight per account for owner actions. Submission without a wallet in the page.

Out of scope: the sponsor policy and error types (E04.01). The Capacity Exchange swap route (`adapter-fee-capacity-exchange`, later). Deploy planning, which is E04.12 (wave deploy).

## Acceptance criteria

- A client with no NIGHT and no DUST lands a contract call on Preview through the adapter.
- After a node refusal the adapter asks the sponsor for a new payment; a test proves the old bytes are never submitted twice.
- Each row of the E04.01 (sponsor policy) action table has a test with a recorded sponsor or node answer.
- A dropped connection during the wait ends in a result or a typed timeout within the bound; it never hangs.
- Two owner actions for one account are sent one after the other, never together.
- A submit at "in a block" returns without waiting for finality, and the caller can still await finality later.
